# What Is an On-Demand Creator Service?

Ride-hailing changed transport not by building better cars, but by making a car available the moment you need one, at a price you see before you get in, without owning a fleet or hiring a driver.

An on-demand creator service does the same for creative work. You don't hire a video team, sign a retainer or hunt for a freelancer. You request the video you need, a matched creator takes it, you see the price before you commit, and you pay for what you ship.

That is what Dalaillama is: **Uber for your creative department.**

## How it works

- **You request.** Describe what the video should do, what the product is, who it's for, the length and the languages. Add product photos or references.
- **You're matched.** The request goes to an active creator who makes videos for brands in your industry, or to one you pick from their profile.
- **You see the price.** The creator sends back a brief with the price on it, usually the same day. Change anything before you commit.
- **You approve, then pay.** Pay a share upfront to start. Approve the storyboard before the film is made. Review the finished ad, and pay the rest on approval.

## Why "on demand" changes the economics

A creative department is expensive because capacity is fixed and demand isn't. You pay for editors, designers and tools every month, whether you need two ads or twenty.

On demand flips that:

- **Variable cost:** you pay per video, not per head. A quiet month costs a quiet month's worth.
- **Elastic capacity:** a launch that needs twenty variations in a week doesn't need hiring. More creators take more requests.
- **The right specialist each time:** a food brand's ad goes to a creator who makes food ads, a fashion launch to one who makes fashion. No single in-house team covers every style.
- **Price before work:** every job is quoted before it starts, so budgets are plans, not surprises.

## Why it's faster than a traditional studio

Dalaillama's creators work in a studio built for AI-assisted production: script, storyboard, scene plan, voice, captions and sound in one guided pipeline. Two parts of it matter most for speed and cost:

- **Shots are planned before they're rendered.** A weak scene is fixed in the storyboard, not after paying to render it, so revisions are cheap.
- **Re-render only what needs it.** A change to one shot re-renders that shot, not the whole film. Tools like Stretch fill in frames, so a slow-motion shot costs a fraction of rendering every second.

That's how creators can quote fast, price fairly, and turn variations around quickly. The techniques are covered in [How to Reduce the Cost of AI Video Production](/blog/reduce-ai-video-production-cost).

## For D2C brands

Most D2C brands don't need a full-time video team. They need a steady flow of ads: launches, variations, seasonal offers, explainers. On demand gives you that flow without the hiring and without the [freelancer hassle](/blog/d2c-video-ads-without-freelancer-hassle). A 60-second video starts from ₹1,999 in India ($20 elsewhere).

## For agencies

Agencies feel the fixed-capacity problem most sharply. A new client means hiring before the revenue is certain; losing one means paying for idle editors.

An on-demand creator service lets an agency:

- **take on more clients without hiring:** overflow work goes to matched creators;
- **cover more categories:** a specialist for each client's industry, not whoever is free in-house;
- **lower the cost per video:** AI-assisted production with per-shot re-rendering cuts the time spent per deliverable;
- **keep control:** briefs, storyboard approvals and the final video sit in one place, ready for client review.

The systems side of producing at volume is covered in [How to Scale AI Video Production](/blog/scaling-ai-video-production).

## For creators

The same model works from the other side. Creators get a studio to produce in, a public profile of delivered client work, regular exposure to brands in the industries they make for, and requests routed to them, so they spend their time making videos instead of looking for clients.

## Is on demand right for you?

It's a good fit if you need video regularly but not enough to justify a full team; if your needs spike around launches and seasons; or if you're an agency that wants capacity without fixed cost.

It's less of a fit for live shoots with a crew, or for work that needs a creative partner embedded in your team every day.
